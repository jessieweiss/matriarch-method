Module 00 — Before You Begin

Before you begin.

The mindset module. Read this before Module 1. It's short on purpose.

Why This Module Exists

Everything else in this course is bottom-up: you don't start with paint colors, you start with your nervous system. This module applies that same logic to the course itself, before you touch the course.

Most people start a course like this already braced to fail it — another program, another binder, another thing that asks more of them than they have. That bracing is the first thing to design out. Not with encouragement. With structure.

This module gives you three working roles to step into, and one tool — the Course Companion — to keep you accountable to yourself as you go. That's it. No worksheets, no quiz. Fifteen minutes, then Module 1.

Three Roles, Not One Identity

You're not becoming a different person to take this course. You're borrowing three professional postures, one at a time, because each one changes what you notice.

01

The Researcher

Your job in Module 1 is to collect data on your own nervous system. Not to interpret it, not to fix it — collect it.

A researcher doesn't judge the data. A room that overwhelms you isn't a flaw in you; it's a finding. Write it down the way you'd log an observation, not a confession. If a question makes you want to explain or apologize for your answer, that's the signal to write it down faster, not slower.

The pile is data.

Every reaction, every avoidance, every room you love without knowing why — data. You're not diagnosing yourself. You're building a file.

02

The Designer

A designer works from constraints, not inspiration boards. Constraints aren't the sad part of the process — they're the part that makes the design actually work for the person living in it.

Your sensory profile, your energy patterns, your clutter tendencies: these aren't obstacles to a beautiful home. They're the brief. A designer who ignores the brief and designs what looks good on Pinterest hands the client a house that doesn't work. You are both the designer and the client here. Don't do that to yourself.

Design also means you build in stages. No designer renovates every room at once. You'll be tempted to. Don't. This course is sequenced bottom-up on purpose — physiological and safety needs before belonging, esteem, or self-actualization — because a home that doesn't meet the floor of the hierarchy can't be fixed by getting the top of it right.

03

The Advocate

This role is for the moments the other two aren't enough — when a family member questions why you need the "weird" chair, or when your own instinct is to override your data because it's inconvenient.

An advocate doesn't argue that your needs are reasonable. They act as if that was never in question, because it wasn't. You don't need to justify a nervous system to the people who live with you, and you don't need to justify it to yourself either. This role exists so you have language ready when you need it, not so you have to build a case each time.

What Accountability Looks Like Here

This is a self-guided course. Nobody is checking whether you finished Module 2. That's the actual risk — not that the material is hard, but that there's no natural friction to stop you from letting it quietly stall.

Two things counter that:

The Course Companion

One tracker, six modules, filled in as you go — not after. It's not a productivity system. It's a record, the same way your sensory data is a record. You'll use it to see, in your own writing, that you're moving.

The Founding Cohort

You're not doing this alongside a faceless cohort of strangers who bought a course. You're one of the people this course is being built with. Say where you are, out loud, in the space. Momentum is contagious in both directions — use that.

Your First Commitment

One sentence, before Module 1.

Open the Course Companion and fill in just the top section: your name, today's date, and one sentence on why you're doing this now. Not your final goal — just the honest reason you're here today.

That sentence is what you come back to on the day the course feels like one more thing on the list. It won't be motivational-poster material. It'll be true, which is more useful.
