The Sensory Profile™

This is an interactive version of your Module 1 self-assessment. Check whatever applies — the page tallies it for you as you go. No single "type" gets assigned here; by the end you'll have a working map of where your nervous system needs the most support.

Each of the 8 sensory systems below gets the same three questions:

Overwhelm — what dysregulates you here. Comfort & Rest — what this system needs to feel like on an ordinary, calm day (your baseline, not a fix). Regulation — what you actively reach for once you're already dysregulated and need to come back down.

All three can be true in the same system at once. That's not a contradiction — that's the actual data.

If more than one person will live in this home, each person fills out their own copy. Don't average or compromise your answers to account for someone else yet — that reconciliation happens later, in your Design Brief (Module 3).
Genuinely don't know? Most checklists include an "I'm not sure yet" option near the bottom. Check it instead of guessing, and a reflection question will appear to help you think it through — or just leave it and come back after paying attention for a few days. You don't need pre-existing self-knowledge to start this.
Your answers save automatically in this browser as you go — check a box, and it's saved. Close the tab and come back later on the same device and browser, and everything will still be here.
Working across devices, or want a permanent copy? Autosave only works in this one browser. Use Save My Profile as a PDF any time to save a fully-formatted copy of everything you've filled in — keep it, print it, or bring it into Module 3.

Vision & Hearing

Same three questions as always: Overwhelm (what dysregulates you here), Comfort & Rest (your ordinary-day baseline), Regulation (what you reach for once you're already dysregulated). For Visual and Auditory, Comfort & Rest is measured a little differently — see below.

For these two systems, Comfort & Rest draws on three qualities from architectural neuroaesthetics: coherence, fascination, and hominess. These aren't a checklist of good traits to hit — they're spectrums, and where you land on each is personal. Two people can both feel completely at home at opposite ends of the same scale.
